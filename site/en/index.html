Two minutes of brushing,
one dragon adventure.

Dragon Brush is a phone game that makes kids ask to brush their teeth. Your kid moves their head and opens wide – and the dragon breathes fire at marshmallows in the corners of the screen. By the time they're toasted, the teeth are clean.

How it works

You brush, they play

The front camera follows your kid's face, and a cute 3D dragon copies every move they make – live.

1

Look at the dragon

Hold the phone in front of your kid. Count down – 3, 2, 1 – and the game begins.

2

Open wide – fire!

Your kid opens their mouth and the dragon breathes fire. They turn their head to aim it at the marshmallow, while you brush the teeth on that side.

3

Four corners, two minutes

One marshmallow in each corner, 30 seconds each. The timer only runs while the fire is on target, and the dragon eats every toasted marshmallow. Then – a party!

Two ways to brush

With a grown-up, or on their own

For little ones – a game you play together. For bigger kids – a guide that walks them through it, step by step.

🐲 Brush with a grown-up

The aiming game

Your kid aims the fire with their head while you brush. That splits the mouth into four zones, so none gets forgotten.

  • Three difficulty levels per kid: on Easy, a marker shows exactly where the fire will land
  • Tilting their head sideways? The dragon gently reminds them: "Head straight!"
  • Face not found? Keep going without the camera, and the dragon aims by itself
🪥 Brush by myself

A personal brushing guide

The phone stands in front of your kid and the dragon brushes along with them – zone by zone, the way kids are taught: small, gentle circles.

  • In every zone: the outside by the cheek, the inside by the tongue, and the chewing part
  • A map of the mouth: stained teeth get clean right before their eyes
  • At the end: brush the tongue, and "spit, don't rinse"
  • The timer pauses if your kid walks away, and waits for them

Questions

What age is it for?

Dragon Brush is for ages 3–10. Little ones play the aiming game while a parent brushes, and older kids can move on to "Brush by myself". It's usually a good idea for a grown-up to check the brushing until about age 7.

Does it cost anything?

No. The app is free and has no ads. Points come only from brushing – there are no real-money purchases.

Does it work on a computer?

It's best on a phone, because the game uses the front camera while your kid brushes. You can open it on a computer, but it's less convenient.

What if my kid won't open wide?

On Easy, a small "o" is enough for the dragon to breathe fire, the targets are big, and a marker shows exactly where the fire will land.

Can I see how it's built?

Yes. All of Dragon Brush's code is public on GitHub.
